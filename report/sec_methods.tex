\section{Methods}

\paragraph{Protocol, and a record of every test read.}
Every choice is made on training rows; the test set is a report card read more than once. One stratified split gives 20,975 training and 8,990 test clients (seed 2607526). Five shared stratified cross-validation (CV) folds give each training client an out-of-fold (OOF) probability from a model that never saw it. Hyper-parameters are chosen by CV AUC, and both cut-offs (maximum $F_1$; minimum 5:1 cost) on these OOF probabilities. Round-1 configurations were scored on 16--18 September and entered in the freeze register on 19 October (46 entries, Table~\ref{tab:a-freeze}); since then \texttt{common.evaluate()} refuses to re-score a registered configuration that has changed. Before that, the dated test log is the guard: besides identical re-runs, only RUAN Hangjing's 30 September re-fits of the plain logit and KNN (same settings) were re-scored. Each configuration thus counts as frozen at its first scoring. The log (\texttt{results/TEST\_SET\_LOG.md}, Table~\ref{tab:a-testlog}) also dates eight diagnostic fits outside the register. None set a model, feature or threshold; the linear support-vector machine (SVM) moved to squared-hinge loss after one such read, but training CV backs the move. \emph{Post-freeze checks} are the 21 October analyses: training five-fold CV, never test-scored, selecting nothing (\texttt{results/POST\_REVIEW\_CHECKS.md}; Tables~\ref{tab:a-paycodes-rates}--\ref{tab:a-rcheck}).

\paragraph{Tuning budgets, nested CV, and how to read the numbers.}
Tuning is like-for-like only for a core six (logit, KNN, pruned tree, forest, XGBoost and multilayer perceptron, MLP). Gradient boosting was tuned on half the training rows, the SVMs on 6,000 rows, and bagging's leaf size and the monotone vector were fixed a priori (Tables~\ref{tab:a-methods}--\ref{tab:a-software}). The grids are small: 9 settings for the forest to 84 for the pruned tree, with two or three values for most parameters; widening them at the edges gains at most 0.0003 for XGBoost and 0.0008 for gradient boosting, and only the forest's leaf size is left open (Table~\ref{tab:a-tuningchecks}). Nested CV re-tunes inside each outer fold, so no score grades its own tuning; for KNN and the logistic family it leaves the frozen choices standing (KNN $0.768 \pm 0.008$ against 0.770; the plain logit kept; Table~\ref{tab:a-nestedcv}). On all training rows (training CV, post-freeze), gradient boosting's grid ranks the frozen setting third, only 0.0008 behind (Table~\ref{tab:a-tuningchecks}); the ensembles were not nested (\S7, iv). \emph{Reading the numbers}: AUC is the chance that a random defaulter scores above a random payer (0.5: chance). Average precision (AP) is the mean precision down the ranked list (no skill: 0.221, the default rate). Brier is the mean squared error of the PD. Cost at $r$:1 is $r \times$ missed defaulters $+$ false alarms, so one unit is one false alarm; a cross-fitted cost sets each fold's cut-off on the other four. ``$+0.0082$ [$+0.0021$, $+0.0143$], 5/5'' is the mean of five fold-paired differences, its 95\% interval, widened because the folds share training rows (Nadeau--Bengio [16]), and the count of positive folds. R refits on the same folds match 13 of 15 Python CV AUCs within 0.005, so the ranking is not a software artefact (Table~\ref{tab:a-rcheck}).
