\section{Methods}

\paragraph{Protocol.}
The cleaned sample is split once, stratified on the target, into 20{,}975 training and 8{,}990 test clients under seed 2607526. Every choice --- hyper-parameters, thresholds, the reference model and the top four --- is made on training rows (5-fold stratified cross-validation). Each configuration was then scored on the test set once and frozen: round 1, 34 variants by 18 September, before the instructor's 30 September comment; RUAN Hangjing's second pass of 30 September, which re-scored two already-frozen configurations under her freeze-before-test protocol; and the nine Platt-recalibrated versions on 19 October --- 46 entries in \texttt{results/frozen\_manifest.json} (Table~\ref{tab:a-freeze}), and \texttt{common.evaluate()} refuses to re-score a changed configuration. The bootstrap and cost curves read the test rows but select nothing. Gradient boosting, bagging, the monotone XGBoost, TreeSHAP, partial dependence and the drop-column refits were added after the proposal (16--18 September) with configurations fixed a priori and each scored once; they are the later rows of Table~\ref{tab:a-methods}. Preprocessing is part of the model: standardisation, one-hot encoding of the three categorical columns and, in the imbalance study, resampling sit inside a scikit-learn \texttt{Pipeline} and are re-fitted on the training part of every fold. Tree models are fitted unscaled (splits are invariant to monotone rescaling). Tuning grids, software versions and the monotone constraint vector are Tables~\ref{tab:a-tuning}--\ref{tab:a-monotone}. Two metrics, deliberately different: hyper-parameters are chosen by cross-validated ROC-AUC, because at tuning time the operating point is unknown, and the cut-off is a later decision on training out-of-fold probabilities (maximum $F_1$ for the default class, or minimum expected cost under an assumed 5:1 false-negative to false-positive ratio) --- ranking is a property of the model, the cut-off of the lender's loss function, and accuracy serves as neither.

\paragraph{Did the first round flatter the cross-validation figures?}
The second pass answers with nested cross-validation, hyper-parameters re-chosen inside each outer fold on training rows only (Table~\ref{tab:a-nestedcv}; reproduced 21 October). KNN gives $0.768 \pm 0.007$ against the plain 5-fold 0.770, and $K = 101$ uniform is re-selected on the full training set; the logistic family gives nested AUC 0.760 for plain, ridge and lasso alike, tied within one standard error, so the 1-SE rule fixed in advance keeps the plain model, and class weighting with Platt recalibration changes the cross-fitted 5:1 cost by $-0.4\%$, below the pre-registered 1\% bar, so it is not adopted. The frozen choices stand.\footnote{RUAN's 30 September refit of the same plain logit gave a test cost of 7,065 at 0.5 and a training out-of-fold cut-off of 0.17 costing 5,141, against 7,063 / 0.15 / 5,126 for the 16 September scoring reported throughout: the refits differ by two false positives, and the out-of-fold cost curve is flat between 0.15 and 0.17 (0.08\%).}

\paragraph{Weighting the loss versus moving the threshold.}
Class-weighted XGBoost is cost-sensitive learning, and on this data it is equivalent to moving the threshold: the weighted and unweighted boosters share a test AUC of 0.7887 and differ by 0.0005 in average precision, while the $F_1$-optimal cut-off shifts from 0.27 to 0.55. The price is calibration, Brier 0.1311 unweighted to 0.1772 weighted, because re-weighting fits a prior that is not the portfolio's; Platt scaling [9] fitted on training out-of-fold scores restores it (0.1311). The nine weighted or resampled models were recalibrated this way, scored once on 19 October and frozen (Table~\ref{tab:a-recal}): weight the decision, not the loss --- or recalibrate.
