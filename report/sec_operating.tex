\section{Choosing the operating point}

A credit model is deployed as a cut-off, not as a ranking, and 0.5 is optimal
only when both errors cost the same, which no lender believes. On the test set
the logistic baseline at 0.5 is 81.5\% accurate, above the 77.9\% majority rule,
yet recovers only 32\% of defaulters, and under a 5:1 cost it runs up 7{,}063
units against 7{,}001 for flagging \emph{every} customer.

\paragraph{Why 5:1, and why a range.} The ratio is an assumption with anchors.
It is the Statlog German Credit cost matrix [5]; our own data imply
more --- a defaulter's median outstanding bill is NT\$19{,}868, so at 50--70\%
loss given default a missed default destroys NT\$10--14k while a false alarm
forgoes about NT\$340 of monthly interest margin, an implied 10:1 to 40:1. We
therefore treat 5:1 as one point of a 1:1--20:1 sweep (Table~\ref{tab:saving};
per-model costs at every ratio: Table~\ref{tab:a-costsens}).

\paragraph{The cut-off is worth more than the model.}
At 5:1 (Table~\ref{tab:saving}), moving the logit's cut-off from 0.5 to the
training out-of-fold optimum 0.15 saves 1,937 [1,604, 2,218] of its 7,063 test
cost units (27\%); replacing it by XGBoost at XGBoost's
own cut-off saves a further 145 [$-$18, 299], an interval that includes zero. At
every ratio above 1:1 the threshold saving grows with the ratio while the model
saving stays in the low hundreds, its interval including zero at 5:1 and again at
20:1 --- where the exercise degenerates: the logit's optimal cut-off is 0.01,
flagging 99.9\% of the book, so the ``saving'' there is the trivial flag-everyone
rule. The ratio is an operational dial, not a model-selection criterion: even the
leading ensemble changes with it (forest 6{,}257 against XGBoost 6{,}277 at 10:1).

\paragraph{Theory and search agree --- if the scores are probabilities.}
Minimising expected cost gives the closed form $t = C_{FP}/(C_{FN}+C_{FP}) =
1/(1+r)$, 0.167 at 5:1; the search over training out-of-fold probabilities
returns 0.15 for both the logit and XGBoost (Table~\ref{tab:saving}). The agreement is a calibration test passed, which is why we
report Brier scores (XGBoost: mean predicted probability 0.220 against prevalence
0.221, Brier 0.131); naive Bayes, Brier 0.203, fails it: its searched cut-off is
0.25, and the closed-form region costs more (5{,}473 at 0.15 against 5{,}337 at
0.25).

\paragraph{Different objectives select different models.} XGBoost for ranking
(test AUC 0.7887, AP 0.5784) and for lowest cost (4{,}981 at 0.15, recall 0.784,
25\% below its own 6{,}642 at 0.5); the monotone-constrained XGBoost for
governance, where nine cost units (4{,}990 at 0.14, 0.2\%) buy a score that can
never fall when a borrower delays another month.
